### Zen 5 (Ryzen 7 9700X): pointer versus index edges, and CSR, in the ablation harness (2026-10-22)

#### Reading

- Index edges are slower than pointer edges in every layout: 13% (24-byte gate) and 14% (32-byte gate, so not the non-power-of-two stride) with per-gate lists, about 5% inside CSR. The loop is latency-bound (edge load, then target gate load, then evaluation); an index adds an address computation to that chain on every edge.
- The gain is CSR (one contiguous edge array, 16-byte gate): with pointer edges it is 8% faster than the current layout overall and 15% faster on the designs of 100k gates or more, faster on 24 of 25 circuits, in the topological placement that `optimize()` produces. With a shuffled placement CSR loses (p/d 1.05), so it must be built after reordering.
- Implication (not implemented): indices for the editable DESIGN form (stable under growth and reordering, the saved form, simple macro side tables); a CSR with pointers built by `optimize()` for SIMULATE mode, in fixed arrays that do not reallocate during simulation. Only measured on this machine and in the harness; the full engine needs its own measurement after a change.

#### Correction: how the harness's d relates to the real engine (measured later on 2026-10-22)

`Circuit.hitlist_mem_layout()` on b15 and b19 (JSON load, as the app loads circuits) shows where the engine's lists actually sit. Before `optimize()` they are scattered (18% of consecutive gates' lists adjacent, 34% going backwards), which is what variant d models. After `optimize()` on a clean heap they are already in gate order (98% adjacent, separated only by 16-byte malloc headers), because `optimize()` copies every list into a fresh allocation in the new order. On a heap fragmented with list-sized holes (24-64 B blocks freed in a scattered pattern, as in a long interactive session) the same `optimize()` leaves only 52% adjacent and 24% going backwards. So the q/d gain above is the gain over an unordered layout; the real engine after `optimize()` is close to q on a clean heap (the remaining gain is removing the headers) and in between on a fragmented one. Packing's value is guaranteeing the ordered layout regardless of heap state; its real-engine gain has to be measured on a scratch build (clean and fragmented heap).

### Propagate: fan-out loop mispredictions (Zen 5, 2026-10-23)

Question: how much of `rx-prop`'s time goes to branch mispredictions, which branch it is, and whether removing it raises Meval/s. All variants were built as scratch copies of `reactor/` (macro flops and packed hitlists included) and timed with `tests/ablation_eval.py` (engine time of `batch_toggle`, 3,000 vectors, 3 repeats per process, best or median as stated, pinned to core 2, interleaved builds) on gate-level netlists (flops as 10-gate networks, `NETLIST_ROOT` pointing at the gate-level copies). Every variant printed the same final-state fingerprint as the current build on every circuit.

#### 1. Where the mispredictions are

`perf record -b` (branch records with a mispredict bit) on c6288, rx-prop, 20,000 vectors: 89% of mispredicted branches are the per-gate fan-out loop's condition (`while profile < end`), almost all on the back edge (one more edge than predicted). The `inputlimit` test in `evaluate()`, the UI-queue test, the macro type test and the outer loop are clean.

| Circuit | rx-prop Meval/s | branch misses per evaluation | share of cycles (at ~18-25 cycles each) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| c6288 | 282 | 0.22 | ~30% |
| c5315 | 290 | 0.20 | ~28% |
| c3540 | 300 | 0.20 | ~30% |
| c1908 | 324 | 0.17 | ~28% |
| b15 (whole run) | 483 | ~0.04 | small |

(ISCAS85 rows from the stored `Benchmark/master_test.md (ISCAS85 section)` PMU table; b15 from `perf record` inside `propagate`.) The loop's trip count is each popped gate's fan-out count; on random logic consecutive pops have different counts (c6288: 40% one edge, 38% two, 19% three) in an order the predictor cannot learn. On regular designs (ITC99, OpenCores controllers) the sequence is far more predictable.

#### 2. What was tried

| Variant | Idea | c6288 | b15 | Outcome |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| current | per-gate loop | 269 ms | 76 ms | baseline |
| pairs + sentinel | pad lists to even length with a shared dummy edge, walk two edges per iteration | 322 ms | 95 ms | slower: the dummy's `inputlimit` made `evaluate()` mispredict instead, and all dummy edges hit one memory location |
| pairs + branchless `evaluate` | as above, select without a branch (needs an `asm` barrier, GCC turns the arithmetic back into a branch) | 278 ms | 108 ms | mispredictions halved (185M to 89M) but 28% more instructions: no gain |
| pairs + copy padding | pad with a copy of the list's last edge, seed 3 (no effect, real cache-hot target) | 264 ms | 101 ms | small gain on c6288, large loss on b15 (65% of its gates have one edge, padding nearly doubles their work) |
| class queues at push | push each changed gate into one of three queues by fan-out (conditional moves per edge) | 174 ms | 110 ms | removes 79% of c6288's mispredictions, but about 12 instructions per edge |
| flat edge stream | expand each wave into one edge list (4 unconditional writes per gate), then one loop | 228 ms | 97 ms | exact (same evaluations); +18% on c6288, geomean 0.90x over 36 circuits |
| fan-out-ordered placement | place gates by (depth, fan-out) in `optimize()` | 275 ms | 78 ms | no effect on prop, sweep 2x slower on c6288 (pop order follows discovery, not addresses) |
| **split at pop** | branch-free split of each wave's popped gates into 1-edge / 2-edge / other arrays; straight-line code for the first two | **130 ms** | 79 ms | big wins on random logic, small losses on regular designs |
| **split at pop, waves of 64+ gates only** | small waves keep the arrival order | **135 ms** | 80 ms | best variant; not adopted (section 4) |

The lesson from the padding and stream variants: each removed mispredict saves roughly 20-25 cycles, but any scheme that adds work per *edge* gives it back, because most gates have one or two edges. Only per-*gate* bookkeeping (the split) pays.

Reading:
* **Random and arithmetic logic gains 1.1-2.0x** (all of ISCAS85, des_area, systemcdes, aes_core, EPFL adder/max/i2c, ITC99 b14/b20/b22). Part of the gain is fewer mispredictions (c6288: 94M to 20M branch misses in the whole run with the class variant), part is fewer evaluations: processing a wave's one-edge gates first suppresses glitches on reconvergent logic (c6288 x0.69, c2670 x0.84, c5315 x0.86). c6288 reaches 392-404 Meval/s against 285.
* **Regular designs lose 3-9%** (b15, b17, wb_dma, wb_conmax, mem_ctrl, usb_phy, ss_pcm, systemcaes, arbiter): they hardly mispredicted, keep their evaluation count, and pay the split plus a less sequential memory order (b15: +6.6% instructions, +12% L1 misses inside `propagate`).
* No static netlist statistic predicted the winner (fan-out distribution, its entropy, and the dynamic "fan-out changes between consecutive pops" rate were all checked; b14 and b15 have near-identical statistics and opposite results).
* Correctness of the split build: final state identical on all 36; `tests/test_timing.py` topologies and extended unchanged; `tests/verify_state.py` (REACTOR_DIR pointing at the build) exact against Icarus on b14, b15, b17, aes_core and mem_ctrl (`--all-clocks`), both modes.

#### 4. Status

Not adopted (author's decision, 2026-10-23). The split was briefly in `reactor/` behind `-DDARION_WAVE_SPLIT` and has been removed: part of its gain comes from the changed pop order suppressing glitches, not from removing mispredictions, it slows regular designs, and it makes evaluation counts depend on the grouping. The measurements above stay as the record. A principled look at evaluation order (avoiding re-evaluation of reconvergent logic) is a separate question. Sweep (`rx-sweep`) mispredicts little (0.08-0.56% of sampled branches on b15 and c5315); scratch variants of it (lazy evaluation, region-skipping scans) traded gains on random logic for losses on sequential designs and were dropped without a record.
